## Per-material

### Sand

- Beside fire or lava, melts to glass readily.
- Soaks up any water it touches readily, becoming dirt once it takes a unit in.
- Dissolves in acid easily.

### Water

- Once conducted heat reaches it, boils into steam fast.
- Wets whatever it touches: things that soak will draw it in.
- Dissolves in acid easily.

### Stone

- Holds heat fast and passes it on instantly, draining back to ambient readily once nothing is heating it.
- Dissolves in acid reluctantly.

### Gas

- Catches fire instantly from fire or lava.

### Fire

- Is a heat source in its own right; sometimes leaves smoke when it burns out. Touched by a quenching liquid, becomes steam.

### Wood

- Catches fire readily from fire or lava, and burns where it stands, rather than flaring away.
- Once alight, burns down fast, and licks flame into an empty neighbour swiftly; often leaves smoke when it burns out. Touched by a quenching liquid, simply goes out.
- Dissolves in acid easily.
- Standing in wet soil, sprouts leaf beside itself readily.
- Once already in leaf and able to reach water, buds new plant beside itself fast.
- Touching water and rooted in soil with room for more, drinks readily - the water comes out as a level of moisture in the soil at its root, not in itself.
- Spending its own soil moisture to grow, bud or sprout, sometimes roots into the soil it drinks from, welding that cell into root.

### Steam

- Warms whatever it touches swiftly, without igniting or quenching anything.
- A 2x2 block of it slowly condenses into a single cell of water.

### Smoke

- Warms whatever it touches fast, without igniting or quenching anything.

### Oil

- Catches fire swiftly from fire or lava, only where it touches air.
- Dissolves in acid barely.

### Lava

- Is a heat source in its own right, and licks flame into an empty neighbour readily. Touched by a quenching liquid, becomes stone.

### Acid

- Once conducted heat reaches it, boils into gas instantly.
- Dissolves an adjacent cell swiftly, sometimes leaving smoke behind.
- Spontaneously evaporates into gas slowly - unconditional, no heat or neighbour required.

### Glass

- Under long heat from fire or lava, melts to lava.
- Holds heat swiftly and passes it on instantly, draining back to ambient readily once nothing is heating it.
- Shatters into sand [TODO: trigger].

### Snow

- Beside fire or lava, melts to water instantly.
- Chills whatever it touches fast.
- Melts in any liquid it touches steadily, becoming water.

### Dirt

- Beside fire or lava, melts to metal readily.
  usually comes out as stone instead, in clumped runs rather than an even speckle.
- Spoils into sand [TODO: trigger].
- Soaks up any water it touches swiftly, growing wetter (its own moisture rises) rather than changing into anything.
- Dries back out readily, on its own, with no partner needed.
- Dissolves in acid easily.

### Ice

- Beside fire or lava, melts to water instantly.
- Chills whatever it touches swiftly.
- Melts in any liquid it touches steadily, becoming water.

### Plant

- Catches fire fast from fire or lava.
- Dissolves in acid easily.
- Grows into wet soil readily, against gravity, spending a level of that soil's moisture per cell.
- Falls under gravity instantly when there is empty space beneath it.
- Withers away slowly if it cannot reach water through its own roots.
- A straight run of 6 cells sometimes hardens into wood, up to 2 cells wider at the foot than at the tip, and usually a limb holds its own direction (rather than bending back toward gravity); the hardened body counts as part of wood.
- The moment it hardens, it also often leafs its crown with leaf, one candidate space at a time.
- Touching water and rooted in soil with room for more, drinks fast - the water comes out as a level of moisture in the soil at its root, not in itself.
- Spending its own soil moisture to grow, bud or sprout, sometimes roots into the soil it drinks from, welding that cell into root.

### Leaf

- Catches fire instantly from fire or lava.
- Dissolves in acid very easily.
- Withers away slowly if it cannot reach water through its own roots and is not touching wood.
- Touching water and rooted in soil with room for more, drinks fast - the water comes out as a level of moisture in the soil at its root, not in itself.

### Metal

- Passes heat on instantly, without banking any of it itself.
- Dissolves in acid barely.

### Root

- Touching lava - and lava only, never a flame - becomes fire fast.
- Dissolves in acid easily.
- Withers away slowly if it cannot reach water through its own roots and is not touching wood.
- Spending its own soil moisture to grow, bud or sprout, rarely roots into the soil it drinks from, welding that cell into root.

## How these sentences are built

Not a markup pass on the table above - that table stays as clean prose, unedited (see the plan's own phasing: it is the deliverable this whole file exists to produce). This section is generated separately, from the same decode helpers (`adverb()`, `to_name()`, `prose_name()`) and the same live `reactions[]`/`extended_reactions[]` rows, picking one representative sentence per group and marking which part of it came from where.

### Legend

Colour means exactly one thing on this page: this word is a material. Every material name below - subject or object, wherever it appears, whether it names a single TARGET field through `to_name()` or is one member of a derived list like `wetting_liquids`/`heat_sources` walked out by `collect_material_list()` (each member keeps its own colour there too, never collapsed into one shared span) - renders in that material's OWN colour, read straight out of `material_palette()` at the representative swatch a freshly painted cell of that material actually takes - mirroring `random_cell()` in sand.c rather than app_sand.c's brush indicator, which still shortcuts every ordinary material to variant 13 (a plausible shade for sand alone, and the wrong colour for any material whose variant is not a shade) - so this list doubles as the key: whatever colour a name gets below is the colour that same name gets in every example after it.

**Materials**

$\textcolor{#D6A663}{\text{Sand}}$, $\textcolor{#10416B}{\text{Water}}$, $\textcolor{#5A6573}{\text{Stone}}$, $\textcolor{#CEEBBD}{\text{Gas}}$, $\textcolor{#FFE363}{\text{Fire}}$, $\textcolor{#5A3D21}{\text{Wood}}$, $\textcolor{#F7FBFF}{\text{Steam}}$, $\textcolor{#84796B}{\text{Smoke}}$, $\textcolor{#101008}{\text{Oil}}$, $\textcolor{#8C1400}{\text{Lava}}$, $\textcolor{#296908}{\text{Acid}}$, $\textcolor{#296984}{\text{Glass}}$, $\textcolor{#E6EFF7}{\text{Snow}}$, $\textcolor{#73593A}{\text{Dirt}}$, $\textcolor{#B5E7F7}{\text{Ice}}$, $\textcolor{#526529}{\text{Plant}}$, $\textcolor{#6BB23A}{\text{Leaf}}$, $\textcolor{#7B8694}{\text{Metal}}$, $\textcolor{#BDA68C}{\text{Root}}$

These are the device's exact palette values, not colours chosen for legibility - deliberately, so the doc and the panel never disagree. That means a few names above sit near the ends of the range on purpose: the palest materials (snow, sand) wash out against a light background, and the darkest (stone) washes out against a dark one. If a name anywhere in this section is hard to read, that is the palette speaking, not a rendering bug.

Nothing else on this page is coloured. The palette answers what colour a material is; it has never answered what colour a verb should be, so the three grammar roles below are marked with typography instead, one weight per role:

- Verb (*italic*) - the wording tied to the field driving the clause, set off with a light touch since it is naming an action, not a substance. `field_docs[]` carries a `verb` string per field for exactly this reason, but phase 1 never actually reads that column back out (grep `->verb` in this file - nothing matches): today the words are typed directly into the matching `emit_*()` printf() call, kept in sync with `field_docs[]` by hand instead of by the compiler.
- Rate / frequency (**bold**) - the ladder bucket: `adverb_for()` for a genuine per-step rate (SCALE_RATE), or `chance_bucket_for()` through frequency_words[]/ease_words[] for a one-shot chance (SCALE_CHANCE). Two different ladders share this one channel because both answer the same question - "which of five bands" - for the same kind of raw byte; only the vocabulary differs, and it is DIFFERENT for a reason (see ease_words[]'s own comment above). Bold makes every rate word scannable in one pass, which is exactly what the by-feel adverb tuning pass still ahead of us (phase 2) needs.
- Cause (***bold italic***) - a trigger that lives at a read site in sand_reactions.c, not in this table - rendered as the literal `[TODO: trigger]` placeholder rather than guessed at. Bold italic marks it as a clause rather than a single word, which is also the rarest thing on this page: every instance today is that same placeholder.
- Glue (plain, unmarked) - prose typed by hand inside the `emit_*()` function itself: connective words, punctuation, the parts no field drives.

## Hand-added behaviour (not generated)

Real mechanics that `dump_reactions.c` cannot derive - each one rides on an existing `reaction_t` field's read site (not the field's stored value itself), or on no field at all, so there is no table row for the generator to walk. See `docs/Sand/Reaction-Doc-Generator-Plan.md`'s phase 2 for the plan to make these generator-derivable via `REACTION_DOC` registration; until then they are documented here, by hand, and are never touched by `report_reactions.sh` (see this file's own top note).

### Water

Touching a heat-holding neighbour above room temperature, drains its banked heat back toward ambient about 8x faster than that neighbour cools on its own - never below ambient, so water cannot chill anything the way snow or ice can.

### Stone

The generated entry above ends "draining back to ambient readily once nothing is heating it." In full: that drain runs about 8x readily instead, for as long as a quenching liquid (water or acid) touches the cell - though still never past ambient.

### Glass

Same mechanic as Stone's note just above, and the same generated sentence shape: "draining back to ambient readily once nothing is heating it" runs about 8x readily instead, for as long as a quenching liquid (water or acid) touches the cell - still never past ambient.

### Lava

- Being quenched into stone (see the generated entry above) chains a small chance into one further neighbouring lava cell, which chains again in turn - up to `SAND_LAVA_COOLOFF_MAX_CHAIN` links per event - which is what lets a sustained pour freeze a pool progressively deeper rather than only sealing its surface.
- Doing the WORK of actually melting a neighbour into something new (not merely banking one more level of heat) costs lava that same small chance of freezing itself, one cell at a time, and chains the same way.
- Covered by at least 3 of the 5 gravity-relative neighbours forming a semi-disc opposite gravity (non-liquid, denser than itself, and forming one contiguous run around that arc - or, axis-aligned only, the three cardinals of it alone even with both flanking diagonals open), rolls a tiny chance per step (1 in 256 - the rarest a single roll can express) to become stone and immediately explode at that spot, fire included - the replacement for lava's old venting behaviour, which no longer exists.

### Dirt

Wet soil runs DOWNHILL as well as sideways and drinking from a puddle - see the generated "Soaks up" entry above - but the downhill share alone is throttled to a separate rate, `SOIL_PERCOLATE_CHANCE` (a `#define` in `sand_reactions.c`, not a `reaction_t` field: dirt is the only material with `dries != 0`, so it is the only one that could ever reach this branch, and a byte on every material's row for something only one of them can use was rejected once already - see `flaw_to`'s own comment in `material.c`). 15 in 256, a quarter of the 60 the downward branch used to run at implicitly (it read the same `soaks` the "Soaks up"/lateral-spread lines above still do) - reported as wet dirt "running down" through dirt too fast, with no separate knob to slow just that. Drinking from an adjacent puddle and spreading sideways between soil cells are unaffected.

### Wood, Plant

Standing in soil with room for more, `.drinks` above puts a level of moisture into the ground the tree stands in - see the generated entry. Spending a level of THAT moisture back out again (growing, budding, or sprouting - see the generated "roots into the soil it drinks from" line) has a further small chance of welding the contact cell - the collar, where the tree actually touches ground - into a `Root` cell instead of leaving it as ordinary dirt: dirt is a powder and shifts, and a root, `KIND_STATIC`, cannot be carried away out from under a tree's collar the way loose dirt can.

This is a ONE-TIME SEED, not a repeating conversion: it fires only while `root_depth == 0` (`spend_soil_moisture()`, `sand_reactions.c`) - meaning `find_water()`'s stem walk crossed no root at all on the way to the collar. The moment the first root exists, this roll never fires again for that tree; everything the root system grows into after that first cell is Root's own row, below, not this one. There is no depth cap here any more (`ROOT_DEPTH_MAX` is retired - see its RETIRED comment in `sand_reactions.c` where the constant used to live) because there is no longer a repeating walk-bound conversion for a depth cap to bound.

A root counts as more STEM for the purposes of `find_water()`'s own walk down to water (so a rooted collar does not cut a tree off from water below it, and a root buried under dirt that shifted back on top of it is transparent to the same walk) but never counts against `TREE_LIFT` - a cell of root is below the water line and lifts nothing, which is the whole reason a root is its own material rather than more wood.

### Root

The generated entry above reads "spending its own soil moisture to grow, bud or sprout, rarely roots into the soil it drinks from" for this row too - and that sentence is WRONG here, not merely incomplete the way Stone's and Glass's corrections above are. `dump_reactions.c` has one template for `roots`/`roots_to`, built for the Wood/Plant reading (see that paragraph above), and Root's own row does not grow, bud, or sprout at all - the generator has no way to know this row means something else by the same field. See `docs/Sand/Reaction-Doc-Generator-Plan.md`'s phase 2 for teaching the generator that distinction directly; until then, read no further than this correction into the generated line for this one row.

What `roots` actually means here: no `grows`, `falls`, `drinks`, `sprouts` or `buds` anywhere on Root's row, but not inert either. `roots` is a SECOND READING of the same field the Wood/Plant paragraph above uses for the one-time seed: on Root's own row it means a root cell rolls, every step, to eat a moist dirt neighbour among its own eight and convert it into more root - `step_one_rooting_cell()`, `sand_reactions.c`. The conversion IS the water cost: the dirt cell's moisture nibble is simply gone along with everything else that cell used to be, once it is no longer dirt at all. Moisture's own shape (percolating down, diffusing sideways) does most of the shaping - a root system spreads wide near a wet surface and fingers downward through a drying bed - with one SMALL skew on top of it, read off the grid with no state: a candidate that continues AWAY from the cell's own root neighbours weighs +2 and one that reaches gravity-ward +1, over a base of 1 (`step_one_rooting_cell()`). A tip has one parent, so it keeps going the way it was going, the same thing `holds_line` does for a stem; a junction's neighbours partly cancel and it is free to turn. The trunk a root grew from counts as a parent as well (`clings_to`), so the first root under a tree heads down from under the wood. Added after the device showed the pure moisture-following rule preferring the sides so strongly that depth only happened at the angles the geometry favoured: measured over thirty seeds, mean deepest root 3.5 rows with the weights zeroed and 7.2-7.4 with them, systems about twice the size, the runaway scene still pinned at a fixed point. Past that the weights are not the depth lever - every saturated bed was 98-99% dry by the end with no live tip beside moist dirt, so how deep the WATER goes is what bounds how deep the roots can (see Sand-Simulation.md's Roots section).

Bounded three ways: the moisture itself (finite, unless something keeps rewatering the bed); `ROOT_SURFACE_MAX` (2, `sand_reactions.c`) - a root already touching more than two other roots does not roll to grow at all, which is what actually keeps the system a filigree rather than a solid block, measured against a continuously-rewatered scene until the count reached a genuine fixed point; and the small chance itself, 8 in 256. See `docs/Sand/Sand-Simulation.md`'s Roots section for the full measurement this figure came from.

Three more things reach a root cell, and the asymmetry between the first two is the point: **fire never** (`flammability` 0 and `heat_chance` 0, both deliberate - a flame that could eat a tree's anchor out from under it would undo the feature), **lava always** (the generated `Root | Lava | Fire` row above, through `reaction_t.melts` - the only door a burning *liquid* opens, and the reason that field exists at all: a root has no variant to bank a heat ramp in the way glass tells fire from lava, so the line is drawn at the source instead), and **rot** (`withers` 1, the slowest this scale expresses): a root that touches nothing of its tree - neither wood nor, byte-exact through its own `roots_to`, another root, so a column under a living tree never rots from the bottom up in a drought - and can no longer reach water withers away, which is what keeps a burnt forest from leaving 7-20 bone-coloured cells per tree in the ground for ever. It holds still where it stands and holds on to what it is part of, and that second half needs no field of its own: `anchored()`'s own walk (`is_kin()`, `sand_reactions.c`) asks whether a candidate cell is either byte-identical to the falling material or shares ITS `clings_to` - and since a root's material is `MAT_EXTENDED`, never `Wood`, it fails that test for Wood (which sets no `clings_to` of its own, so only an exact match counts) exactly as it fails it for Plant and Leaf (whose `clings_to` names `Wood`, not root). A trunk standing on its own root therefore reports anchored immediately, the same as standing on bare ground, without a root ever needing to be treated as more of the tree's own body - `clings_to = Wood` on the row above describes what a root IS PART OF, not what makes anchoring see through it.

### Pairwise reactions

The generated `Lava | Water / Acid | Stone` row's note column is blank; in full, that reaction chains onward into one further adjacent Lava cell, itself becoming Stone, at `SAND_LAVA_COOLOFF_CHANCE`, up to `SAND_LAVA_COOLOFF_MAX_CHAIN` links - see Lava's own entry above.
